Lead Testing Methods

XRF Testing

Best for:

Property lead inspections and paint surveys

  • Detects lead in painted surfaces
  • Provides instant results with no lab analysis required
  • Non-destructive testing, unlike paint chip sampling

Dust Wipes

Best for:

Rental certifications and clearance testing

  • Measures the amount of lead dust on surfaces
  • Samples analyzed by accredited laboratory
  • Confirms whether lead levels meet regulatory standards

Why Lead Testing Matters?

$10,000+

Non-Compliance Fines

Federal and local regulations require lead compliance for rental properties, child-occupied facilities, and certain renovation activities. Failure to meet these requirements can result in significant fines, project delays, and potential legal liability.

500,000+

Children Affected

Lead exposure is especially harmful to young children and pregnant individuals. Even low-level exposure can cause developmental delays, learning difficulties, and long-term health effects.

Pre-1978

Lead in Homes

Before the federal ban in 1978, lead-based paint was widely used in construction due to its durability, moisture resistance, and vibrant color properties. Many properties built before 1978 still contain lead-based paint beneath layers of newer paint.

Ingestion & Inhalation

How Exposure Happens

Lead exposure commonly occurs through ingestion of dust or paint chips and inhalation of airborne particles. This is especially likely when paint is deteriorating or disturbed during renovation or repair work.

Hidden Hazard

Not Always Visible

Lead-based paint is often hidden beneath layers of newer paint. Even well-maintained homes can contain hazardous levels of lead in surfaces or settled dust. Professional testing is the only way to confirm its presence.
