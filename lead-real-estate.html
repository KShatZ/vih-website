Maryland Rental Lead Inspections

V-IH Solutions provides lead inspections, dust wipe sampling, and certificate testing for rental properties across Maryland. Whether you need Full Risk Reduction, Lead Free, or Limited Lead Free certification, we help property owners and managers navigate the inspection process, maintain compliance, and keep properties ready for occupancy.

The Maryland Department of the Environment seeks to reduce the risk of lead exposure, particularly among young children, while ensuring rental properties are maintained in safe condition. As a result, most rental properties built before 1978 must be registered with the Maryland Lead Program unless exempt from the program requirements, such as properties that have obtained a Lead Free Certificate.

Lead Inspection & Certification Services

  • Full Risk Reduction Inspections

    We perform Full Risk Reduction inspections for initial property certification as well as tenant turnover compliance. Each inspection begins with a visual evaluation of all interior and exterior painted surfaces. If the visual inspection passes, dust wipe samples are collected and submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis. Inspection results and required documentation are then submitted to both the MDE and the property owner.

  • Lead Free & Limited Lead Free Inspections

    Using our SciAps X-550 XRF analyzer, we perform non-destructive lead paint inspections to determine whether properties qualify for Lead Free or Limited Lead Free certification. Surface-by-surface testing is performed throughout the property with detailed reporting documenting all inspection findings. Results and required documentation are submitted to the MDE upon completion of the inspection. If no lead-based paint is identified, the property may qualify for exemption from Maryland lead program requirements.

  • Re-inspection & Ongoing Compliance Support

    We work with landlords and property managers to maintain ongoing compliance requirements for registered rental properties. This includes Limited Lead Free visual re-inspections, repeat Full Risk Reduction inspections, failed clearance re-testing, and coordination around tenant turnover timelines.

Understanding Maryland Lead Certificates

Dust Wipe + Visual

Full Risk Reduction Certificate

What it Means:

A Full Risk Reduction certificate is the most common compliance path for Maryland rental properties built before 1978. The property may still contain lead-based paint, but painted surfaces must be in acceptable condition and lead dust levels must meet Maryland clearance standards throughout the property.

How Testing Works:

The inspection includes a visual evaluation of all interior and exterior painted surfaces along with lead dust wipe sampling from floors, windowsills, and window troughs. Samples are analyzed by an accredited laboratory to confirm dust levels are below MDE limits.

Ongoing Obligations:

Full Risk Reduction certificates must be renewed at each tenant turnover by passing the inspection again. Maintaining paint condition and proper cleaning prior to testing are critical for passing inspection and avoiding delays in occupancy.

XRF Inspection

Lead Free Certificate

What it Means:

A Lead Free certificate confirms that no lead-based paint was detected throughout the property. Once certified Lead Free, the property is permanently exempt from Maryland lead program requirements. This provides long-term peace of mind and eliminates the need for future tenant turnover inspections.

How Testing Works:

Using XRF technology, painted surfaces throughout the property are tested individually to determine whether lead-based paint is present. Testing is non-destructive and provides immediate results on-site.

Ongoing Obligations:

Once a property receives a valid Lead Free certificate, no future lead inspections or re-certification requirements apply under the Maryland lead program. This exemption remains with the property and can significantly reduce long-term compliance costs and administrative burden.

XRF + Visual

Limited Lead Free Certificate

What it Means:

A Limited Lead Free certificate is issued when no lead-based paint is detected on interior surfaces, but lead remains present on exterior components or certain exterior areas cannot be fully tested. The property becomes exempt from interior lead testing requirements, however exterior painted surfaces remain subject to ongoing maintenance and re-inspection requirements.

How Testing Works:

Interior painted surfaces are tested using XRF technology while exterior painted components are visually evaluated for deteriorated paint conditions. The property may qualify if interior areas are confirmed lead free and exterior painted surfaces remain properly maintained.

Ongoing Obligations:

Limited Lead Free properties require certificate renewal every two years through visual re-inspections of exterior painted surfaces. Paint conditions must remain intact and free of deteriorated lead hazards.

Inspection & Certification Process

  1. Step 1

    Initial Consultation

    Contact us to discuss your property, certificate needs, and compliance timeline. We'll help determine which inspection type is required based on your property's status, occupancy, and lead certification history.

    1. Step 2

      Property Registration & Scheduling

      Rental properties built before 1978 must be registered with the Maryland Lead Program prior to inspection. Once registration is confirmed and a valid MDE Property Tracking number has been obtained, we coordinate required MDE notifications and schedule the inspection around your turnover or project timeline.

    2. Step 3

      On-Site Inspection & Testing

      Depending on the certificate type, the inspection may include visual evaluation of painted surfaces, XRF lead paint testing, and/or lead dust wipe sampling. Most inspections are completed within a few hours depending on property size and scope.

    3. Step 4

      Laboratory Analysis & Reporting

      Dust wipe samples are submitted to an accredited laboratory for analysis while inspection findings are compiled into detailed documentation and reporting. Once laboratory results are received, all required paperwork is finalized, submitted, and incorporated into the final inspection report.

    4. Step 5

      Certificate Issuance & Next Steps

      Final inspection documentation and certificates are submitted to both the MDE and the property owner. If ongoing compliance is required or the property did not pass inspection, we'll explain future re-inspection timelines and next steps for maintaining or obtaining certification.

FAQ

What is an MDE Tracking Number and how do I get one?

An MDE Tracking Number is a unique identification number assigned to a rental property through the Maryland Lead Program. Rental properties built before 1978 must be registered with the program before lead inspections and certifications can be performed. Property owners can register online through the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) and will receive a tracking number once registration is completed.

Do all rental properties need lead inspections?

Most rental properties built before 1978 are required to comply with Maryland lead laws unless the property qualifies for an exemption, such as holding a valid Lead Free or Limited Lead Free certificate. Depending on the property's certification status and tenant turnover activity, inspections and testing may be required periodically to maintain compliance.

How often do I need to get testing done?

Testing frequency depends on the certificate type associated with the property. Full Risk Reduction certificates require re-certification at each tenant turnover, while Limited Lead Free certificates require visual re-inspection every two years. Properties with a valid Lead Free certificate are exempt from future lead inspection requirements under the Maryland lead program.

What happens if I rent without a valid lead certificate?

Renting a property without a valid lead certificate can result in violations, fines, and potential legal liability under Maryland law. In some cases, owners may also face difficulties with tenant disputes, enforcement actions, or lead-related claims. Maintaining an active certificate helps demonstrate compliance and protects both property owners and occupants.

What happens if my property fails inspection?

If a property does not pass inspection, the next steps depend on the reason for failure and type of inspection performed. Common issues include deteriorated paint conditions or elevated lead dust levels. After the identified issues are corrected and the property is properly cleaned, re-testing can be scheduled to obtain compliance certification.

Can I switch from Full Risk Reduction to Lead Free?

Yes. Many property owners initially maintain compliance through Full Risk Reduction certification and later pursue Lead Free certification through abatement, renovation, or replacement of lead-containing materials within the property. Obtaining a Lead Free certificate requires comprehensive XRF testing throughout the property to confirm that no lead-based paint is present. Once certified Lead Free, the property becomes exempt from ongoing Maryland lead inspection requirements.
