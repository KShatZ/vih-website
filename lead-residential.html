Residential Lead Testing & Inspection

Whether you're buying, renovating, or raising a family in an older home, professional lead testing can provide clarity and peace of mind. Our certified inspectors perform detailed surface-by-surface inspections to identify potential lead hazards in painted surfaces, household dust, and surrounding soil.

When Residential Lead Testing Makes Sense

Living in a Pre-1978 Home

Many older homes contain lead hidden beneath newer layers of paint. Testing provides peace of mind and helps identify potential hazards in your environment.

Children or Pregnant Family

Young children and pregnant individuals are especially vulnerable to the harmful effects of lead exposure.

Planning Renovations

Renovations can disturb lead paint and send hazardous dust throughout your home. Testing beforehand helps reduce lead exposure risks during and after renovation work.

Peeling or Deteriorating Paint

Chipping, peeling, or damaged paint in older homes can release hazardous lead dust and increase exposure risks inside the home.

Previous Lead Findings

Follow-up testing can help confirm whether previous lead hazards have been properly addressed.

Buying an Older Home

A lead inspection before closing can uncover hidden hazards and help you make a more informed purchasing decision.

Residential Lead Testing Services

XRF Testing

Lead Paint Inspections

Our inspectors use XRF (X-ray fluorescence) technology to test painted surfaces throughout your home and determine whether lead-based paint is present. Every accessible surface is analyzed individually, giving you a clear understanding of where potential lead hazards may exist within the property.

  • Immediate on-site results
  • Non-destructive testing methods
  • Surface-by-surface inspection process
  • Detailed reporting of tested surfaces throughout the property
Dust Wipes & Soil

Lead Exposure Testing

Even when lead paint appears intact, lead dust can accumulate on floors, windowsills, and other surfaces over time. Dust wipe and soil sampling measure the amount of lead present within your living environment, helping determine whether lead hazards are actively affecting your home.

  • Interior dust wipe sampling
  • Accredited laboratory analysis
  • Soil testing available
  • Post-renovation and exposure testing

FAQ

Will lead testing damage my walls?

No. Modern lead testing methods such as XRF are completely non-destructive. In the past, paint chip sampling was commonly used, where small pieces of paint were removed from surfaces for laboratory analysis. Today, we use a handheld XRF lead analyzer to determine whether lead is present without damaging painted surfaces or opening walls. Dust wipe and soil sampling are also minimally invasive and do not cause damage to your property.

Do I need to leave my home during testing?

You do not need to leave your residence during testing. Lead inspections and testing can typically be performed while the home is occupied. We do require access to all areas of the property and ask that painted surfaces, windows, and other testing areas are easily accessible during the inspection.

How long will testing take?

The length of testing depends on the size of the property and the scope of work being performed. Most residential inspections can be completed within a few hours. XRF testing provides immediate on-site results, while dust wipe and soil samples require laboratory analysis which typically takes a few business days.

What happens if lead is found?

If lead is detected, we'll clearly explain where it was found, the potential level of concern, and what your options are moving forward. In many cases, lead-based paint can be safely managed if it remains in good condition and is not being disturbed. If elevated dust or deteriorating paint conditions are identified, we may recommend remediation, repairs, or additional precautions depending on the situation.

Can children be exposed without visible paint damage?

Yes, lead exposure can occur even when paint appears intact. Over time, normal wear and friction around windows, doors, stairs, and other painted surfaces can create microscopic lead dust that is not always visible. Young children are especially vulnerable because lead dust can settle on floors, toys, and other surfaces they frequently come into contact with.

Should I test before starting renovations?

Yes, especially in homes built before 1978. Renovation activities such as sanding, scraping, cutting, or demolition can disturb lead-based paint and release hazardous dust into the home. Testing beforehand helps identify whether lead is present so proper precautions can be taken before work begins.
